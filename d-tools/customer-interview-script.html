            Customer interview questions, briefly

            What the script above is built on, if you want to adjust it or write your own.

            What makes a good customer interview question?

            It asks about something that already happened. "Tell me about the last time…" gets a real story with real details. "Would you…?" and "Do you think…?" ask people to predict their own behavior, and people are kind and optimistic about the future, so the answers mislead you.

            How many customer interviews do I need?

            Five to eight per type of person is usually enough to see a pattern. If the same story keeps coming up by the fifth interview, you have it. If every interview sounds different, you may be talking to more than one audience, and each needs its own five.

            How long should a customer interview be?

            Thirty minutes suits most interviews. Fifteen works for people who barely used your product or are doing you a favor. Forty-five is for when you want to see their workaround on screen or hear the whole buying process.

            Should I show them my idea?

            Not until the end, and only if you're testing a concept. Once they've seen it, everything they say is about your idea rather than their problem. If you don't yet know whether the problem is real, start with the Project Validation Methods tool to choose the right test.

            How it works

            1. Pick what you need to learn: whether the problem is real, whether your idea fits, whether people will pay, why customers stay, or why they left.
            2. Say who you're talking to. Strangers, friends and customers each need a different opening and different care.
            3. Describe the people and the problem in their words. Every question is written around them.
            4. Pick a length. Fifteen minutes keeps the essential questions; forty-five adds the deeper follow-ups.
            5. Leave with a timed script, the questions to avoid, and what would tell you to keep going or rethink. Copy it, save it as text, or download a PDF.
