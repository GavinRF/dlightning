A map for work that connects people

Building a United Community

Four systems that, set in place together, create a united community: individual experience, the experience design cycle, systems thinking and playful learning design. Each overlap is where art and science combine, and the center is the outcome they build toward.

The map, written out

Four specialties, the four places where they meet, and the center they build toward.

Specialty

Individual Experience

Perception, emotion and experience, from the personal to the cultural, translated into products that are meaningful and useful.

Perception

What people notice first, what they miss, and what they assume a thing does before they touch it.

Emotion

How each step feels: the confidence after something works, the doubt before a payment, the relief when an error explains itself.

Personal to cultural

Experience is shaped by one person’s history and by the shared language, norms and symbols of the groups they belong to. Both decide what a design means to someone.

Translation

Turning what is learned about people into features, words and flows they can use, so the insight survives the trip into the product.

Questions to ask

  1. What does this person already believe when they arrive?
  2. Where do they hesitate, and why?
  3. What would this mean to someone from a different background?

Specialty

Experience Design Cycle

A non-linear way of gathering data, finding insights and testing solutions, grounded in empathy for the customer’s perspective.

Gather data

Interviews, observation, analytics and support logs, collected to see the problem from the customer’s side. Watch what people do as closely as you listen to what they say.

Find insights

Sort the raw material into patterns: the needs that keep coming up, the workarounds people invent, the moments where things go wrong.

Test solutions

Put a prototype in front of real people early and cheaply, measure what changed and keep what holds up.

Non-linear

A test can send you back to research, and one interview can reshape a solution. The cycle goes where the evidence points, not in a fixed order.

Questions to ask

  1. What have we assumed that we haven’t checked?
  2. Which finding would change our plan if it turned out to be true?
  3. Who haven’t we talked to yet?

Specialty

Systems Thinking

Awareness of a system’s inputs and outputs, from messaging and data points to actions and pipelines, and how they add up to a whole greater than the sum of its parts.

Inputs

Everything that enters the system: a user’s action, a marketing message, a data feed, a support ticket, a decision made in a meeting.

Outputs

Everything it sends back out: screens, emails, notifications and reports, plus the habits and expectations it builds in the people who use it.

Pipelines and loops

How outputs become inputs again. A confusing email produces support tickets, the tickets produce a help page, and the help page changes the next email.

The whole

People judge a product as one thing. A well-made screen inside a broken flow still reads as a broken product.

Questions to ask

  1. What feeds into this, and what does it feed?
  2. If we change this piece, what else moves?
  3. Where does information get lost between teams or tools?

Specialty

Playful Learning Design

Progressively difficult puzzles, rewards, collected loot, leveling up, building teams and making personal connections: the things that produce joy and carry people along their journey.

Progressive challenge

Each puzzle a little harder than the last, so people stay just past what they can already do without tipping into frustration.

Rewards and loot

Rewards triggered by real progress and things worth collecting along the way, so effort leaves something behind.

Leveling up

Growth people can see: where they started, where they are now and what opens up next.

Teams and connection

Building teams and making personal connections, so the journey is shared and people come back for each other as much as for the product.

Questions to ask

  1. What is the next thing this person is almost able to do?
  2. What do they get to keep from the effort they put in?
  3. Who are they doing this with?

Where Individual Experience meets Playful Learning Design

Personalized Growth Path

Playful learning shaped around one person: the challenge, the pacing and the rewards fit what they have done, how they feel about it and where they want to go.

Starts where they are

The path begins from what the person already knows and cares about, not from step one for everyone.

Paced to the person

Difficulty rises at the rate each person can take. Someone breezing through gets harder puzzles; someone stuck gets a different way in.

Rewards that land

What counts as a reward depends on the person: recognition, mastery, a collectible or time with a team. The path learns which one works.

Questions to ask

  1. How does the path change for someone who arrives already skilled?
  2. What happens when a person gets stuck?

Where Individual Experience meets Experience Design Cycle

Inclusive Design Process

The design cycle run with the full range of people who will use the product, so perception, emotion and culture count as evidence from the start.

Who’s in the room

Research and testing include people across ages, abilities, languages and cultures, not a single stand-in for an average user.

Edges inform the middle

Designing for someone using a screen reader, a slow connection or a second language usually makes the product clearer for everyone else too.

Empathy with evidence

Feelings and cultural context get recorded and tested like any other finding, so they shape decisions instead of staying in a slide deck.

Questions to ask

  1. Whose experience is missing from our research?
  2. Would this still work in another language, on another device, with another ability?

Where Playful Learning Design meets Systems Thinking

Dynamic Exploration Framework

Systems built for exploring: clear rules, responsive feedback loops and room to experiment, so people learn how things work by playing with them.

Rules you can discover

The system behaves consistently enough that people can form a theory, test it and find out they were right.

Feedback that responds

Every action produces a visible result, so cause and effect are easy to read and curiosity gets an answer.

Room to experiment

Safe ways to try something, undo it and try again. Mistakes cost little, so people take the risk of exploring.

Questions to ask

  1. Can someone figure out the rules by trying things?
  2. What does a mistake cost here?

Where Experience Design Cycle meets Systems Thinking

Holistic Product Development

The design cycle applied to the whole system: research, insight and testing cover messaging, data, pipelines and operations, not one screen at a time.

Research the whole journey

Study the path across every touchpoint: the ad, the signup, the product, the receipt, the support call.

Test across the seams

Problems often live where one team’s output becomes another’s input. Test those hand-offs as carefully as the screens.

Measure the system

Data from every part of the system feeds the next round of insight, so the cycle keeps going after launch.

Questions to ask

  1. Which hand-off between teams has nobody tested?
  2. What does the customer see that no single team owns?

The center

United Community

A mutual understanding of high-level goals and rules of engagement, defined internally, so that people can work together.

Shared goals

Everyone can say what the work is for, in the same words. Goals are set high enough to cover the whole product and plainly enough that a new teammate can repeat them on their first day.

Rules of engagement

How decisions get made, who gets asked, how disagreement is settled and what done looks like. The team writes them itself, so people follow them because they agreed to them.

Defined internally

The community forms inside the team before it forms around the product. The understanding a team builds for itself is the one it ends up offering the people it serves.

What feeds it

Each specialty brings something to the center. Individual experience brings empathy, the design cycle brings evidence, systems thinking brings a view of the whole and playful learning brings the reasons people stay.

Questions to ask

  1. Could each person on the team state the goal without looking it up?
  2. When two people disagree, does everyone know how it gets settled?
  3. Would a new member know the rules by the end of their first week?

Altogether

How the map fits together

Four specialties

Individual Experience shows what one person notices, feels and brings from their culture. The Experience Design Cycle turns that into evidence you can test. Systems Thinking widens the view to everything that person touches, and Playful Learning Design gives them reasons to keep going.

Where two meet

An Inclusive Design Process brings the full range of people into the cycle. Holistic Product Development runs the cycle across the whole system. A Dynamic Exploration Framework makes the system something to play with, and a Personalized Growth Path shapes the play around one person.

The center

All four feed a United Community. It starts inside the team, with goals everyone can say in the same words and rules everyone agreed to.
